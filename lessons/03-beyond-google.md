# Lesson 03: Beyond Google

**Goal:** know the other search engines and big free knowledge sites, and when
each one beats Google.

**Time:** about 40 minutes.

---

## Why look beyond Google?

Google is good, but it's a business. Its first results are often ads, big
brands, or pages built to rank well rather than to teach well. More and more
often, the very top is an AI-written answer instead of a link (Lesson 01). Other tools can
show you different corners of the web, and many are made by non-profits whose
only goal is to share knowledge.

You don't have to replace Google. Just keep a few other tools in your pocket.

---

## Part 1: Other search engines

| Search engine | Why use it |
|---|---|
| [DuckDuckGo](https://duckduckgo.com) | Doesn't track you. Has **"bangs"**: type `!w photosynthesis` to search Wikipedia directly, or `!yt` for YouTube. There are thousands of bangs. |
| [Bing](https://www.bing.com) | A completely separate index from Google, so it sometimes finds things Google misses. Supports most of the same operators (`site:`, `filetype:`, quotes, `-`). |
| [Brave Search](https://search.brave.com) | Has its own index, private, with a "Discussions" section from forums. |
| [Startpage](https://www.startpage.com) | Google's results, but without Google tracking you. |
| [Mojeek](https://www.mojeek.com) | A small independent search engine with its own index. Different results. |
| [Marginalia Search](https://marginalia-search.com) | Finds small, personal, non-commercial websites: hobbyists and experts writing for love, not ad money. Great for deep, quirky knowledge. |

**Habit to build:** if Google gives you junk twice in a row, try the same search
on one other engine before giving up.

### AI answers on other search engines too

Bing and DuckDuckGo can also put an AI-written answer above the links. The same
rule applies as on Google: it can be wrong, so check the pages it links to
(SIFT, Lesson 00). Here's how to hide it:

- **Bing** calls its AI answers **Copilot**. Add `-ai` to the end of your search,
  for example `photosynthesis -ai`. Unlike on Google, this is an official Bing
  feature, made by Microsoft. Bing has no setting to switch AI answers off for
  good.
- **DuckDuckGo** calls its AI answers **Search Assist**. To switch them off: open
  DuckDuckGo's **Search Settings**, then **AI Features**, choose
  **Never** next to "Search Assist", and click **Save and Exit**. Or simply search
  at [noai.duckduckgo.com](https://noai.duckduckgo.com): it's normal DuckDuckGo
  with every AI feature turned off, and AI-made pictures removed.

## Part 2: Wikipedia, used properly

Teachers sometimes say "don't use Wikipedia." Better advice: **don't stop at
Wikipedia.** Use it as a map.

- **Read the top summary** to get the basic idea and the right words to search
  with (Lesson 00).
- **Scroll to "References" and "Further reading."** These are the real sources:
  books, papers, official reports. Click them. Many are free.
- **Check the "Talk" tab** at the top of an article to see what editors argue
  about. It tells you which parts are disputed.
- **Switch language** (top right). The article in another language is sometimes
  much longer or has different sources.

Wikipedia's sister projects, all free:

| Site | What it is |
|---|---|
| [Wikibooks](https://www.wikibooks.org) | Free textbooks written by volunteers. |
| [Wikisource](https://wikisource.org) | Free original texts: old books, laws, speeches, historical documents. |
| [Wikiversity](https://www.wikiversity.org) | Free learning materials and courses. |
| [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org) | Millions of free-to-use photos, diagrams and videos. |
| [Wiktionary](https://www.wiktionary.org) | A dictionary for every language, with word origins and pronunciations. |

## Part 3: Free books and libraries online

| Site | What it has |
|---|---|
| [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org) | 70,000+ free classic books whose copyright has ended. |
| [Standard Ebooks](https://standardebooks.org) | Public-domain classics, beautifully formatted for e-readers. |
| [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org) | A catalogue of millions of books; some readable or borrowable for free. |
| [HathiTrust](https://www.hathitrust.org) | Millions of scanned library books; public-domain ones are free to read in full. |
| [Google Books](https://books.google.com) | Search **inside** books. Many show free previews; old ones are fully readable. Click **Tools → Free Google eBooks**. |
| [WorldCat](https://www.worldcat.org) | Search the catalogues of libraries around the world to find which library near you has a book. |
| [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu) | Millions of free items from European museums, archives and libraries. |
| [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov) | Free historical photos, maps, newspapers, recordings. |

## Part 4: Question-and-answer communities

| Site | Use it for |
|---|---|
| [Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.com) | Programming questions. Probably already answered: search before asking. |
| [Stack Exchange](https://stackexchange.com/sites) | 170+ Q&A sites on maths, cooking, languages, DIY, money, travel, and more. |
| [Hacker News search](https://hn.algolia.com) | Search years of discussion among tech people on any tool or idea. |
| Reddit | Covered fully in Lesson 05. |

**Search trick:** these sites are well indexed by Google, so you can do
`site:stackoverflow.com <your error message>` or
`site:stackexchange.com <question>`.

## Part 5: Tools that answer questions directly

- **[Wolfram|Alpha](https://www.wolframalpha.com):** type a maths problem, a unit
  conversion, a science fact or "population of Kenya vs Uganda" and it
  calculates the answer. The free version is very capable.
- **AI chatbots:** helpful for explaining a topic simply or suggesting search
  words. **But they make things up**, including fake book titles, fake quotes
  and fake references, and sound very sure while doing it. Treat an AI answer
  as a *starting point*, then confirm it with a real source using the SIFT
  method from Lesson 00. The same goes for the AI answers at the top of Google,
  Bing and DuckDuckGo.

---

## Part 6: Fact-checking sites

When you see a claim going viral, check here first:

- [Africa Check](https://africacheck.org): fact-checks claims in Africa.
- [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com): long-running fact-checker of internet rumours.
- [Full Fact](https://fullfact.org): UK-based fact-checker.
- **Reverse image search** with [Google Lens](https://lens.google),
  [TinEye](https://tineye.com) or Bing Visual Search: find out where a photo
  really came from and when it first appeared.

## Exercise

1. Do the **same search** on Google, DuckDuckGo and Marginalia. Write down one
   good result that appeared on one engine but **not** the others. Did any of
   them start with an AI answer? Try hiding it (on Bing, add `-ai`).
2. Open the Wikipedia article for your topic. Scroll to "References" and find
   one **free** source listed there. Open it.
3. Find one **free classic book** related to your topic on Project Gutenberg,
   Standard Ebooks, or Google Books (full view).
4. Ask an AI chatbot for "3 good books about <your topic> with authors."
   Then check each one **really exists** using WorldCat or Google Books. Did any
   turn out to be made up?
