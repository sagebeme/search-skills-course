# Lesson 07: Free, Legal Learning

**Goal:** know where to learn almost anything for free, legally, and how to tell
a great free resource from a weak one.

**Time:** about 45 minutes.

---

## The big idea

Before paying for any course, ask: **"Who already teaches this for free?"**
Universities, non-profits, governments, software makers and passionate
teachers give away excellent material. A lot of paid courses are built on top
of it.

---

## Part 1: Free online courses (MOOCs)

A **MOOC** ("massive open online course") is a full course, often from a real
university, that anyone can join online.

| Site | What's free | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org) | Everything | Maths, science, economics, computing. Non-profit. All levels from primary school up. |
| [MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu) | Everything | Real MIT lecture notes, exams, and many videos. |
| [OpenLearn](https://www.open.edu/openlearn) | Everything, including free statements of participation | From the UK's Open University. Hundreds of short courses. |
| [Saylor Academy](https://learn.saylor.org) | Courses and certificates | Self-paced college-level courses. |
| [freeCodeCamp](https://www.freecodecamp.org) | Everything, including certifications | Coding and data, learn by doing. Non-profit. |
| [The Odin Project](https://www.theodinproject.com) | Everything | Full web development path. |
| [CS50 (Harvard)](https://cs50.harvard.edu) | Everything | Famous intro to computer science; free on its site, YouTube and edX. |
| [edX](https://www.edx.org) | Usually an **audit** option | Audit = watch and read for free, but graded work and the certificate cost money. Audit access may expire. |
| [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org) | Varies | Many courses used to have a free **audit**; free access has been shrinking and some courses now only offer a short preview. Look for "Audit" or "Preview". **Financial aid** is available on many courses: you apply with a short form. |

> **About certificates:** most employers care more about what you can **show**
> (a project, a portfolio, a test) than a paid certificate. Learn free first.
> Pay only if a certificate is truly needed, and check for financial aid.

## Part 2: Open textbooks

An **open textbook** is a full, proper textbook that's free to read, download
and print, legally, because its authors chose an open licence.

| Site | What's there |
|---|---|
| [OpenStax](https://openstax.org) | Peer-reviewed university textbooks: biology, physics, economics, statistics, psychology and more. |
| [LibreTexts](https://libretexts.org) | Huge library of open textbooks in science, maths, humanities. |
| [Open Textbook Library](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks) | Hundreds of open textbooks, many with reviews from teachers. |
| [Siyavula](https://www.siyavula.com) | Free maths and science textbooks for secondary school (South African curriculum). |
| [African Storybook](https://www.africanstorybook.org) | Free children's stories in many African languages. |
| [Wikibooks](https://www.wikibooks.org) | Volunteer-written textbooks (quality varies). |

**Search recipe** (from Lesson 01):

```
"open textbook" <subject>
"creative commons" textbook <subject> filetype:pdf
site:openstax.org <subject>
```

When you see **Creative Commons** (often shortened to **CC BY**) on a book, it
means the author has given permission to share it freely.

## Part 3: Libraries, now on your phone

Your public library may give you free e-books and audiobooks through an app.

- **[Libby](https://libbyapp.com)** (by OverDrive): borrow e-books and
  audiobooks with a **library card** from a library that offers it.
- **[Hoopla](https://www.hoopladigital.com)**: similar, offered by some
  libraries.
- **Ask your local or national library** if they offer **digital membership** or
  an e-library. Some let you sign up online. Some university libraries also
  offer public or alumni access.
- **Open Library** and **Project Gutenberg** (Lesson 03) need no library card.

Search: `"<your country or city>" library e-books digital membership`

## Part 4: Official documentation

For technology, the **official documentation** ("docs") is written by the people
who made the tool. It's free, current and correct, and many paid courses just
read it to you.

| Topic | Free official docs |
|---|---|
| Web (HTML, CSS, JavaScript) | [MDN Web Docs](https://developer.mozilla.org) |
| Python | [docs.python.org](https://docs.python.org/3/tutorial/) (start with "The Python Tutorial") |
| Many languages in one place | [DevDocs](https://devdocs.io) |
| Microsoft tools (Excel, Azure, etc.) | [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com) (free learning paths too) |
| Google tools | [Google Help Centers](https://support.google.com) and developer docs |
| Almost any software | Search `<tool name> official documentation getting started` |

**Tip:** docs often have a **"Getting started"** or **"Tutorial"** section made
for beginners. Start there, not with the reference pages.

## Part 5: YouTube, used well

YouTube has complete university courses for free, if you can find them among
the noise.

### Search tricks

- Add words like `full course`, `lecture 1`, `playlist`, `for beginners`.
- Use **Filters** (after searching): set **Type → Playlist** to find whole courses,
  and **Duration → Over 20 minutes** to skip short clickbait.
- Search for a university's channel: `MIT OpenCourseWare`, `Stanford`,
  `Harvard CS50`, `NPTEL` (Indian Institutes of Technology).
- Search Google instead: `site:youtube.com "lecture 1" <subject> playlist`.

### Good channels to start with

- **Khan Academy**, **CrashCourse** (short overviews of many subjects),
  **3Blue1Brown** (maths explained visually), **freeCodeCamp.org** (long free
  coding courses), **MIT OpenCourseWare**, **NPTEL**, **TED-Ed**.

### Judging a YouTube teacher

- Do they **show their work** or just talk confidently?
- Do **comments** from learners say it helped or point out mistakes?
- Do they keep pushing you to buy their **"real" course**? Fine, but the free
  video should still teach something real on its own.
- Check the **upload date** for fast-changing topics like software.

## Exercise

Pick your topic (the same one you've been using) and find:

1. One **complete free course** (MOOC, OCW, or a YouTube playlist).
2. One **open textbook** or free book.
3. One **official documentation** page or official guide (if your topic is not
   technical, find an official body's guide instead, e.g. a health ministry, a
   bar association, a national exams council).
4. Check whether **your library** (local, national or university) offers free
   e-books. Write down what you found out.
5. Find one **paid** course on your topic. Using everything above, list the free
   resources that together cover the same material.
