# Lesson 04: Telegram

**Goal:** find good public learning channels and groups on Telegram, search
inside them, and judge whether they can be trusted.

**Time:** about 40 minutes. Have the Telegram app open (phone or desktop).

---

## Quick words first

- **Channel:** a one-way feed. Only the owners post; you read. Like a
  noticeboard or newsletter.
- **Group:** a chat where everyone can talk. Like a classroom discussion.
- **Public** channels and groups have a username (like `@examplechannel`) and
  anyone can find and join them. **Private** ones need an invite link.
- **Bot:** an automatic account that replies to commands.

> **Before you start: protect yourself.** In **Settings → Privacy and Security**:
> - Set **Phone Number** to **Nobody** so strangers in groups can't see it.
> - Set **Groups & Channels** ("who can add me") to **My Contacts** so you
>   can't be thrown into scam groups.
> - Turn on **Two-Step Verification** (a password on top of the SMS code).

---

## Part 1: Finding public channels and groups

### Method 1: Telegram's own search

1. Tap the **search bar / magnifying glass** at the top of your chat list.
2. Type a topic word: `python`, `english practice`, `biology`, `excel`.
3. Scroll past your own chats. You'll see a **Global search** (or "Channels")
   section with public channels and groups matching the name.

Tips:

- Telegram mostly matches **names and usernames**, not what's posted inside.
  So try short, simple topic words, and try **several versions**: `learnpython`,
  `python_tips`, `python dev`.
- Try other languages too: `aprender inglés`, `kiswahili`, etc.

### Method 2: "Similar channels"

After you join a channel, Telegram often shows **similar channels** that people
who joined it also follow. One good channel leads you to others.

### Method 3: Search Telegram through Google

Public channels have web pages at `t.me/<username>`. Google indexes them, so
you can use your Lesson 01 skills:

```
site:t.me "data science" course
site:t.me/s "machine learning"
site:t.me intitle:<topic>
```

**Bonus:** you can read most public channels in a normal web browser, without
joining or even having an account, by visiting
`https://t.me/s/<username>`. This is a great way to look around **before**
joining.

### Method 4: Follow trusted links

The safest channels are the ones linked **from an official website**. A
university, a well-known teacher, an open-source project or a news outlet
often lists its Telegram channel on its own site. Search:

```
"<organisation name>" telegram channel
site:<organisation-website> t.me
```

If the link comes from the organisation's real website, you know the channel
is really theirs.

## Part 2: Searching inside a channel or group

Once you're in a channel with years of posts, don't scroll forever.

- **Search messages:** open the chat, tap the **name at the top** or the
  **⋮ / three-dots menu → Search**. Type a word to find every message with it.
- **Search by person (groups):** in the chat search, filter by **a member** to see
  only what they posted. Useful for finding what the admins or experts said.
- **Jump to a date:** tap the **calendar** icon in chat search to go straight to
  a certain day. Useful to see when the channel started.
- **Hashtags:** many channels tag posts (`#beginner`, `#exercise`). Tap a
  hashtag to see every post with it.
- **Pinned messages:** the bar at the top of a chat. Admins pin rules, guides
  and the best resources there. Always read them first.
- **Media tabs:** tap the channel name, then look at the **Media / Files /
  Links** tabs to see everything shared, sorted by type.

## Part 3: Is this channel trustworthy?

Telegram is full of fake channels, bought followers and scams. Check these
things **before** you trust anything a channel shares.

### Green flags

- It's **linked from an official website** of a real organisation or person.
- It has a **blue verified tick** (only given to some official accounts).
- It's been around a long time: use the calendar to jump to its **first post**.
- **Views look natural** compared with subscribers. A channel with 20,000
  subscribers usually gets a few thousand views per post.
- There's **real discussion**: comments or a linked group with real questions
  and answers.
- Posts link to **sources you can check**.
- It **gives** you things: explanations, free official links. It doesn't ask
  for anything.

### Red flags

- A **"SCAM"** or **"FAKE"** label next to its name. Telegram adds these after
  reports. Leave immediately.
- **Huge subscriber count but tiny views** (100,000 subscribers, 300 views).
  The subscribers were probably bought.
- A name that **copies** a famous brand or teacher, with a slightly different
  username (`@harvard_courses_free`, `@khanacademyy`).
- **"DM the admin"** to get the resource, or "pay a small fee to unlock".
- Posts that are only **files to download** (`.apk`, `.exe`, `.zip`, `.rar`),
  especially "premium course free" or "cracked app". These are very commonly
  used to spread malware. Lesson 06 covers this.
- **Crypto**, "investment", "earn $500 a day" mixed in with "learning".
- **Comments turned off** and no way to ask questions.

> **Tip:** sites such as [TGStat](https://tgstat.com) show public statistics for
> many channels (growth over time and average views). A sudden jump of
> thousands of subscribers in one day is a sign of bought followers.

### The golden rules

1. **Telegram staff will never message you asking for your login code.** Anyone
   who does is stealing your account.
2. **Never send the code Telegram texts you to anyone**, even a "friend" who
   says they sent it by mistake. Their account was probably already stolen.
3. When a channel shares a link to a free course, **check the link goes to the
   real site** (e.g. `edx.org`, not `edx-free-courses.xyz`) before you click.
