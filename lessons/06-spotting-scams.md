# Lesson 06: Spotting Scams and Fake Info

**Goal:** recognise the three most common traps for people looking for free
learning: fake "free course" channels, dangerous downloads, and phishing links.

**Time:** about 40 minutes.

---

## Why this matters

Scammers know that people who want to learn but can't afford to pay are
hopeful and in a hurry. "Free premium course!" is one of the most common bait
lines online. Losing your Telegram, email or M-Pesa account, or having your
phone infected, costs far more than any course.

The good news: almost every scam has the same few warning signs.

---

## Trap 1: Fake "course" channels and pages

### How it works

A channel or page promises a famous paid course for free, or "certificates
guaranteed", or a "scholarship". Then it asks you to:

- pay a small "registration" or "unlock" fee,
- send your phone number, ID or login details,
- install an app, or
- "invite 10 friends to unlock the next lesson."

### Warning signs

- The name **copies a famous brand** with small changes (`Couresra`, `@edx_free_official1`).
- **Urgency:** "Only 20 spots left!", "Offer ends tonight!"
- **"DM the admin"** to receive the material.
- Real organisations offering free things **never** ask for a fee to unlock
  them.
- **Guaranteed jobs** or **guaranteed certificates** for no work.

### What to do instead

Go **directly** to the real website (type it yourself or search for it, don't
click the channel's link) and see if the offer exists there. Real free courses
are announced on the real site.

---

## Trap 2: Malware in "free download" files

**Malware** is any program made to harm you: stealing passwords, bank or M-Pesa
access, or locking your files and demanding money.

"Free" versions of paid courses, paid apps, games and software are one of the
most common ways malware spreads, because the person downloading **wants** to
open the file and will ignore warnings to do it. This is one big reason this
course doesn't cover finding pirated material: it's where the dangerous files
live.

### Dangerous file types

| File type | What it is | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| `.exe`, `.msi`, `.bat`, `.scr` | Windows programs | Runs code on your computer. **High risk** from unknown sources. |
| `.apk` | Android apps | Can take over your phone, read your SMS (including bank codes). **High risk** outside the Play Store. |
| `.zip`, `.rar`, `.7z` | Packed folders | Can hide any of the above inside. |
| Password-protected `.zip`/`.rar` | Locked packed folder | **Very suspicious.** The password stops your antivirus from checking inside. |
| `.docm`, `.xlsm` | Office files with "macros" | Asking you to "Enable content" or "Enable macros" is a classic trick. |
| `.pdf` | Document | Usually fine from official sites. Be careful with PDFs from strangers. |

### Tricks to recognise

- **Double extensions:** `Python_Course.pdf.exe`. It looks like a PDF but it's a
  program. Turn on "show file extensions" in your computer's settings so you
  can see the real ending.
- **"Your download is ready, disable your antivirus first."** Never.
- **Fake download buttons:** pages full of big green "DOWNLOAD" buttons that are
  actually ads.
- **"You need this player / codec / installer to open the video."**

### What to do

1. **Prefer files from official sites** (`.edu`, `.gov`, the creator's own site).
2. Only install apps from the **official app store**.
3. If you must check a file, upload it or paste its link at
   [VirusTotal](https://www.virustotal.com). It scans with dozens of antivirus
   tools at once. (Don't upload private documents: uploaded files can be seen
   by security researchers.)
4. Keep your phone and computer **updated**.

---

## Trap 3: Phishing links

**Phishing** means a fake page or message pretending to be someone you trust,
so you'll type your password, code or payment details into it.

### How to read a web address

The real owner of a link is the part **just before the first single `/`**,
reading the last two pieces of it:

| Link | Who really owns it |
|---|---|
| `https://www.coursera.org/learn/python` | coursera.org (real) |
| `https://coursera.org.free-courses.xyz/login` | **free-courses.xyz** (fake) |
| `https://courserra.org/login` | **courserra.org** (fake, extra r) |
| `https://login-coursera.com` | **login-coursera.com** (fake, not Coursera's site) |

Also watch for:

- **Shortened links** (`bit.ly/...`) hiding the real address. Be careful when
  they come from strangers.
- **Letters that look alike:** `rn` vs `m`, `0` vs `o`, `l` vs `I`.
- A page asking you to **log in** after you clicked a link in a message. Close
  it and go to the site yourself.

### The Telegram / WhatsApp code scam

"Hi, I sent you a code by mistake, please forward it to me." Or: "Vote for my
cousin in this competition, enter your number and code." **That code is the key
to your account.** Never share it with anyone, for any reason.

### SMS and M-Pesa style scams

"You've received money by mistake, please send it back", or a fake message from
"customer care" asking for your PIN. Real companies **never** ask for your PIN.
Check your actual balance in the official app or menu, not the link or number
in the message.

---

## Fake information (not just fake links)

Remember **SIFT** from Lesson 00. Extra signs of fake info:

- **Screenshots of "news"** with no link to the original article.
- **Old photos shared as new.** Use reverse image search (Lesson 03).
- **"Doctors don't want you to know..."** or "they're hiding this from you".
- **Numbers with no source.**
- **An AI answer at the top of a search page.** It can be wrong, and it doesn't
  say who wrote it. Open the pages it links to and check those (Lesson 01).
- It perfectly confirms what you already believed. That's exactly when to
  check harder.

---

## If something goes wrong

1. **Change your password** for the affected account from a safe device, and
   turn on two-step verification.
2. **Telegram:** Settings → Devices → **Terminate all other sessions**.
3. **Money:** call your bank or mobile money provider using the number on their
   **official** website or card, straight away.
4. **Infected device:** disconnect from the internet, run an antivirus scan, and
   if in doubt, back up your photos/documents and reset the device.
5. **Report** the channel, account or page on the platform so others are
   protected.
